## Thomas Watson — A Body of Divinity (1692)  (48)

1. [ ] The best way for Christians to be settled is to be well grounded.

2. [ ] If all we receive is from his bounty, is it not reasonable we should glorify him?

3. [ ] When we praise God, we spread his fame and renown, we display the trophies of his excellency.

4. [ ] Oh let this be the thing we are chiefly ambitious of, the enjoyment of God in his ordinances!

5. [ ] The devil and his agents have been blowing at Scripture light, but could never blow it out; a clear sign that it was lighted from heaven.

6. [ ] Canst thou say it is of divine inspiration, because thou hast felt it to be of lively operation?

7. [ ] Though a thousand men behold the sun, there is light enough for them all: put never so many buckets into the sea, there is water enough to fill them.

8. [ ] To think God is in this place would add wings to prayer, and oil to the flame of our devotion.

9. [ ] Sin tempts with its pleasure; but, when we think of eternity, it may cool the intemperate heat of lust.

10. [ ] All the treasures of wisdom are locked up in him, and no creature can have any wisdom but as God is pleased to give it out of his treasury.

11. [ ] At Rome there were two laurel trees; when the one withered, the other flourished.

12. [ ] The longer God is in taking his blow, the heavier it will be at last.

13. [ ] All the mercy in the creature is derived from God, and is but a drop of this ocean.

14. [ ] Some drops of love may run beside to the creature, but the full stream must run towards God.

15. [ ] Plato was convinced of a Deity when he saw that all the world could not make a fly.

16. [ ] Providence feeds the young raven, when the dam forsakes it, and will give it no food.

17. [ ] Is it not irrational to lose heaven for the satisfying or indulging of lust?

18. [ ] Satan knew a temptation coming to Adam from his wife would be more prevailing, and would be less suspected.

19. [ ] As whatever the leper touched became unclean; such a leprosy is original sin; it defiles our prayers and tears.

20. [ ] You hope to be kings to reign in heaven, and will you let Satan reign in you now?

21. [ ] Christ takes away the enmity in us, and the wrath of God, and so makes peace.

22. [ ] Life must be parted with shortly; what is it to part with it a little sooner, as a sacrifice to Christ, as a seal of sincerity, and a pledge of thankfulness!

23. [ ] The Spirit helps us with sighs and groans; not only with gifts but groans.

24. [ ] Though our lusts are too strong for us, they are not for Christ to conquer; for by his Spirit he can break the power of sin.

25. [ ] What wise man would damn himself to grow rich? or pull down his soul, to build up an estate?

26. [ ] Examine if your hearts be an unclean fountain, sending out the mud and dirt of pride and envy.

27. [ ] A man is convinced of sin, he sees he is a sinner and nothing but a sinner; the fallow ground of his heart is broken up.

28. [ ] Our adoption was purchased at a dear rate; for when God was about to make us sons and heirs, he could not seal the deed but by the blood of his own Son.

29. [ ] We are naturally polluted, and to cleanse, God takes to be his prerogative.

30. [ ] A hypocrite may leave sin, yet love it; as a serpent casts its coat, but keeps its sting; but a sanctified person can say he not only leaves sin, but loathes it.

31. [ ] A letter may be written, when it is not sealed; so grace may be written in the heart, and the Spirit may not set the seal of assurance to it.

32. [ ] Men grow rich by trading; so by trading in grace we grow rich in assurance.

33. [ ] The wicked may have something which looks like peace, but it is not.

34. [ ] Joy is setting the soul upon the top of a pinnacle - it is the cream of the sincere milk of the word.

35. [ ] Pray that God would make you grow in grace, though it be by affliction.

36. [ ] Let your knowledge of God be clear, and serve him purely out of choice, and then you will persevere.

37. [ ] Death will set him out of gunshot, and free him from sin and trouble.

38. [ ] What hurt does death, but take us from among fiery serpents, and place us among angels?

39. [ ] These are not pillars in the temple of God, but reeds shaken every way.

40. [ ] We shall not only have God's glorious presence at certain special seasons; but we shall be continually in his presence, continually under divine raptures of joy.

41. [ ] If two men owe you money, you may, without any injustice, remit the debt to one, and exact it of the other.

42. [ ] Some think that when Isaac went abroad into the fields to meditate, it was in the book of the creatures.

43. [ ] It is the Trojan horse, that has sword and famine, and pestilence, in its belly.

44. [ ] When conscience accuses, when sickness approaches (which is but a harbinger to bespeak a lodging for death), then what will you do?

45. [ ] So, no sooner does a child of God offend, and God begin to be angry, but immediately Christ steps in and intercedes.

46. [ ] Shall not they who are ennobled with grace have more fragrance in their lives than sinners?

47. [ ] Nothing can have a greater force upon the heart, to sanctify it, than faith.

48. [ ] At death the saints will arrive at perfection; their knowledge will be clear; their sanctity perfect; their sun will be in its full meridian splendour.

## Samuel Rutherford — Letters (1664)  (33)

49. [ ] The world, and the things of the world, Madam, is the lover ye naturally affect beside your own husband Christ.

50. [ ] We may indeed think, Cannot God bring us to heaven with ease and prosperity?

51. [ ] Nay, the interest of a forgotten bond runneth up with God to interest upon interest.

52. [ ] If I found but cold comfort in my sufferings, I would not beguile others; I would have told you plainly.

53. [ ] Not only by them, but through them, must we go; and wiles will not take us past the cross.

54. [ ] If He have the guiding of my flock and state, it shall not miscarry.

55. [ ] I recommend to you prayer and watching over the sins of your youth; for I know that missive letters go between the devil and young blood.

56. [ ] Do nothing but that which ye may and would do if your eye-strings were breaking, and your breath growing cold.

57. [ ] Walk in love, and do righteousness: seek peace; love one another.

58. [ ] Be kind and loving to your wife: make conscience of cherishing her, and not being rigidly austere.

59. [ ] I lay inhibitions upon my thoughts, that they receive no slanders of my only, only Beloved.

60. [ ] I shall be loath to put you off your fears, and your sense of deadness: I wish it were more.

61. [ ] O that I might preach His beauty and glory as once I did, and my branches be watered with the dew of God, and my joy in His work grow green again and bud and send out a flower!

62. [ ] The sanctified cross is a fruitful tree, it bringeth forth many apples.

63. [ ] Ye eat and drink, but time standeth not still; ye laugh, but your day fleeth away; ye sleep, but your hours are reckoned and put by hand.

64. [ ] Learn to know that which the apostle knew, the deceitfulness of sin.

65. [ ] I would fain have that which ye and others believe I have; but ye are only witnesses to my outer side, and to some words on paper.

66. [ ] The best regenerate have their defilements that will clog behind them all their days; and, wash as they will, there will be filth in their bosom.

67. [ ] Keep your garments clean, as ye would walk with the Lamb clothed in white.

68. [ ] Well were my soul, if Christ were the element (mine own element), and that I loved and breathed in Him, and if I could not live without Him.

69. [ ] Oh, how rare a thing it is to be loyal and honest to Christ, when He has a controversy with the shields of the earth!

70. [ ] My removal from my flock is so heavy to me, that it maketh my life a burden to me; I had never such a longing for death.

71. [ ] Put Christ to the bank, and heaven shall be your interest and income.

72. [ ] Ye are taught to know and adore His sovereignty, which He exerciseth over you, which yet is lustred with mercy.

73. [ ] If this storm were over, you must prepare yourself for a new wound; for, five thousand years ago, our Lord proclaimed deadly war betwixt the Seed of the Woman and the seed of the Serpent.

74. [ ] I dare not conceal His goodness to my soul; He looked fremed and unco-like upon me when I came first here; but I believe Himself better than His looks.

75. [ ] There are curtains to be drawn by in Christ, that we never saw, and new foldings of love in Him.

76. [ ] Oh that there were such an heart in you, as to think what an ill conscience will be to you, when ye are upon the border of eternity, and your one foot out of time!

77. [ ] Deliver the Gospel off your hand, and your ministry to your Master with a clean and undefiled conscience.

78. [ ] Sir, I wish your soul may be more acquainted with the sweetness of Christ.

79. [ ] I can say more of Christ now by experience (though He be infinitely above and beyond all that can be said of Him), than when I saw you.

80. [ ] I would give over the bargain a thousand times, were it not that Christ's free grace has taken our salvation in hand.

81. [ ] And who can tell what thoughts of love and peace our Lord has to your children?

## Charles Spurgeon — Morning and Evening (1865)  (46)

82. [ ] Eternal Spirit, our effectual Comforter, we who are the temples in which thou dwellest, will never cease from adoring and blessing the name of Jesus.

83. [ ] Our work for the Lord, its emulation, selfishness, carelessness, slackness, unbelief, what a mass of defilement is there!

84. [ ] Haste, gather up thy wants, and bring them here--thine emptiness, thy woes, thy needs.

85. [ ] Beloved, while we do not neglect external things, which are good enough in themselves, we ought also to see to it that we enjoy living, personal fellowship with Jesus.

86. [ ] All the bread your soul has eaten has come down from heaven, and all the water of which it has drank has flowed from the living rock--Christ Jesus the Lord.

87. [ ] If we have faith as a grain of mustard seed, salvation is our present and eternal possession.

88. [ ] When we serve our Lord Jesus as believers should do, our God is with us; and though we have the whole world against us, if we have God with us, what does it matter?

89. [ ] If thou rememberest that thou art going to heaven, thou wilt not sleep on the road.

90. [ ] You belong to Jesus--obey him; let his word be your law; let his wish be your will.

91. [ ] The broken stones of the law cry out against us, but Christ himself, who has rolled away the stone from the door of the sepulchre, speaks for us.

92. [ ] Do you tell me that the warm blood of youth leaps in your veins? then, I entreat you, give your vigour to the Lord.

93. [ ] Jesus is the angel of the covenant, and if his presence be now earnestly sought, it will not be denied.

94. [ ] We live in the world of death and sorrow, we may therefore look for ills as well in the night-watches as beneath the glare of the broiling sun.

95. [ ] Surely, if he in his wisdom sees fit now to chasten thee, thou shouldst not complain.

96. [ ] Upon his breastplate he is now bearing our names; and in his authoritative pleadings at the throne he remembers our persons and pleads our cause.

97. [ ] His omnipotence, omniscience, omnipresence, immutability and infallibility, are all combined for our defence.

98. [ ] God forbid that any of us should leave our Bibles to become swift witnesses against us in the great day of account.

99. [ ] All his thoughts, emotions, actions, utterances, miracles, and intercessions, were for us.

100. [ ] Think little of yourselves, but do not think too little of your callings.

101. [ ] What God has divided, let us never try to unite, but as Christ went without the camp, bearing his reproach, so let us come out from the ungodly, and be a peculiar people.

102. [ ] Within a very little time thou shalt be rid of all thy trials and thy troubles.

103. [ ] Let us gird up the loins of our mind, and be not weary in well doing.

104. [ ] The toils of devoted missionaries and fervent ministers shame us if we sit still in indolence.

105. [ ] Why should the member have a brain of its own when the head fulfils for it every intellectual office?

106. [ ] Whatever foes may be before the Christian, they are all overcome.

107. [ ] In his written Word thou hast this assurance in part fulfilled, for holy Scripture is his counsel to thee.

108. [ ] Like the two pillars of the temple, they are for glory and for beauty.

109. [ ] Shall his foes have mirth and joy, and shall his home-born children inherit sorrow and wretchedness?

110. [ ] Pain breaks not the calm of the sweet twilight of age, for strength made perfect in weakness bears up with patience under it all.

111. [ ] While it is an application to divine wealth, it is a confession of human emptiness.

112. [ ] In the family register of glory the small and the great are written with the same pen.

113. [ ] Selfishness defiles our tears, and unbelief tampers with our faith.

114. [ ] If you would see the sun, would you gather together the common means of illumination, and seek in that way to behold the orb of day?

115. [ ] Heaven and earth in midnight stillness heard the groans and sighs of the mysterious Being in whom both worlds were blended.

116. [ ] Sleep departed from his eyes, for all night he was in prayer wrestling for his people.

117. [ ] Let the worst come to the worst, let all the talents go, we have not lost our treasure, for that is above, where Christ sitteth at the right hand of God.

118. [ ] You are poor and needy, but he has thought upon you, and has the exact blessing which you require in store for you.

119. [ ] O blessed Spirit, let us hear the death-cries of Jesus, and our hearts shall be rent even as men rend their vestures in the day of lamentation.

120. [ ] There is not a high blessing too lofty for thee, nor a wide mercy too extensive for thee.

121. [ ] We have had deep draughts; we have thought that we could take in all of Christ; but when we have done our best we have had to leave a vast remainder.

122. [ ] The petitions of Moses discomfited the enemy more than the fighting of Joshua.

123. [ ] Some esteem it a small thing for a believer to be vexed with doubts and fears, but God thinks not so.

124. [ ] If our hands are not clean, let us wash them in Jesus' precious blood, and so let us lift up pure hands unto God.

125. [ ] Nay, in every bone, joint, and muscle, sinew, gland, and blood-vessel, you mark the presence of a God working everything according to the design of infinite wisdom.

126. [ ] His love was indeed stronger than the most terrible death, for it endured the trial of the cross triumphantly.

127. [ ] It was by his own supernatural power that he found himself an upper room in which to keep the feast.

## Charles Spurgeon — All of Grace (1886)  (21)

128. [ ] See how this Scripture accepts the charge, and plainly states it!

129. [ ] If the highest court in the universe has pronounced me just, who shall lay anything to my charge?

130. [ ] The wrath of God against iniquity, whatever that may be, must be beyond all conception terrible.

131. [ ] If you yield yourself up to His divine working, the Lord will alter your nature; He will subdue the old nature, and breathe new life into you.

132. [ ] Faith is the root of obedience, and this may be clearly seen in the affairs of life.

133. [ ] Certainly faith does for us what nothing else can do; it gives us joy and peace, and causes us to enter into rest.

134. [ ] If there be this turning, you have the essence of true repentance, even though no alarm and no despair should ever have cast their shadow upon your mind.

135. [ ] However other things may seem to contradict it, will you believe it?

136. [ ] A better plan is this--note the authority upon which you are commanded to believe, and this will greatly help you to faith.

137. [ ] Is it not glorious that the Lord of all is the Saviour of sinners?

138. [ ] Keep these blessed things in their places, and view them in their relation to each other.

139. [ ] Observe with much comfort that the Lord Jesus Christ gives this repentance to the most unlikely people in the world.

140. [ ] Like butterflies they flit from flower to flower, till they visit all the beauties of the garden, and settle upon none of them.

141. [ ] The Lord is able, not only to save us from hell, but to keep us from falling.

142. [ ] Do not attempt to touch yourself up and make yourself something other than you really are; but come as you are to Him who justifies the ungodly.

143. [ ] You have seen a lobster, perhaps, which has fought with another lobster, and lost one of its claws, and a new claw has grown.

144. [ ] Truly, a heart which hungers and thirsts after Christ has but to know that He is freely given, and at once it will receive Him.

145. [ ] If I walk across my study it is because I believe my legs will carry me.

146. [ ] You may not make a Christ out of your repentance, but you must look for repentance to Christ.

147. [ ] I believe that there is such a country as Japan; I never saw it, and yet I believe that there is such a place because others have been there.

148. [ ] If we had the tongues of men and of angels, if we did not receive fresh grace, where should we be?

## Andrew Murray — With Christ in the School of Prayer (1885)  (23)

149. [ ] Though in its beginnings prayer is so simple that the feeblest child can pray, yet it is at the same time the highest and holiest work to which man can rise.

150. [ ] O the blessed liberty and simplicity of a child that Christ our Teacher would fain cultivate in us, as we draw near to God: let us look up to the Father until His Spirit works it in us.

151. [ ] In the names of father and child he has the pledge that his petitions will be granted.

152. [ ] The Lord Jesus was Himself the truth; everything He spake was the deepest truth.

153. [ ] Alas! how many prayers are wishes, sent up for a short time and then forgotten, or sent up year after year as matter of duty, while we rest content with the prayer without the answer.

154. [ ] Faith is fellowship; I give myself up to the influence of the friend who makes me a promise, and become linked to him by it.

155. [ ] Without such voluntary separation, even from what is lawful, no one will attain power in prayer.

156. [ ] The bond that unites a man to his fellow-men is no less real and close than that which unites him to God: he is one with them.

157. [ ] The destinies of the world were given into the power of the wishes, the will, the prayer of man.

158. [ ] I have this day again heard words from Thee which pass my comprehension.

159. [ ] Not as if to the faith he has the obedience must be added, but faith must be made manifest in obedience.

160. [ ] I was more occupied with my speaking to Thee than Thy speaking to me.

161. [ ] The use of the name rests on the unity of life; the Name and the Spirit of Jesus are one.

162. [ ] With every breath we expel the impure air which would soon cause our death, and inhale again the fresh air to which we owe our life.

163. [ ] I would follow Thee to Gethsemane, there to become one with Thee, and to abide in Thee as Thou dost unto the very death yield Thy will unto the Father.

164. [ ] Praying without ceasing is the earthly manifestation of heaven come down to us, the foretaste of the life where they rest not day or night in the song of worship and adoration.

165. [ ] The prayer can exert that influence only when the child is really living in that relationship, in the home, in the love, in the service of the Father.

166. [ ] If it be merely for my own comfort or joy I seek His grace, I do not receive.

167. [ ] The Saviour does not hint that because the Father knows what is best He may give us something else.

168. [ ] If God was only one Person, shut up within Himself, there could be no thought of nearness to Him or influence on Him.

169. [ ] Creation exists to show forth His glory; all that is not for His glory is sin, and darkness, and death: it is only in the glorifying of God that the creatures can find glory.

170. [ ] Give me the opened ear of the learner, wakened morning by morning to hear the Father's voice.

171. [ ] And now, all ye who have anew entered the school of Christ to be taught to pray, take these lessons, practise them, and trust Him to perfect you in them.

## William Law — A Serious Call to a Devout and Holy Life (1729)  (25)

172. [ ] The greatness of those things which follow death makes all that goes before it sink into nothing.

173. [ ] As, therefore, all kinds of dishonesty destroy our pretences to an honest principle of mind, so all kinds of pride destroy our pretences to an humble spirit.

174. [ ] You will hear what plays were acted that week, which is the finest song in the opera, who was intolerable at the last assembly, and what games are most in fashion.

175. [ ] If you may be vain in one thing, you may be vain in everything; for one kind of vanity only differs from another, as one kind of intemperance differs from another.

176. [ ] We are to praise God with words and prayers, because it is a possible way of glorifying God, who has given us such faculties, as may be so used.

177. [ ] I answer, that I am afraid it is one of the most general characters in life; and that few people can read it, without seeing something in it that belongs to themselves.

178. [ ] If Negotius was asked what it is which he drives at in life, he would be as much at a loss for an answer, as if he was asked what any other person is thinking of.

179. [ ] Thou that didst cleanse the lepers, heal the sick, and give sight to the blind, cleanse my heart, heal the disorders of my soul, and fill me with heavenly light.

180. [ ] Every reason for one is in all respects as strong a reason for the other.

181. [ ] If not, let it be now your care to join yourself to that number of devout people, to that society of saints, amongst whom you desire to be found when you leave the world.

182. [ ] How many people swell with pride and vanity, for such things as they would not know how to value at all, but that they are admired in the world!

183. [ ] Whatever way of life we intend them for, we apply to the fire and vanity of their minds, and exhort them to everything from corrupt motives.

184. [ ] As true religion is nothing else but simple nature governed by right reason, so it loves and requires great plainness and simplicity of life.

185. [ ] I desire nothing, I press nothing upon you, but to make the most of human life, and to aspire after perfection; whatever state of life you choose.

186. [ ] By intercession is meant a praying to God, and interceding with Him for our fellow-creatures.

187. [ ] The first followers of Christ seem to support all their love, and to maintain all their intercourse and correspondence, by mutual prayers for one another.

188. [ ] The whole nature of virtue consists in conforming to, and the whole nature of vice in declining from, the will of God.

189. [ ] People of fine parts and learning, or of great knowledge in worldly matters, may perhaps think it hard to have their want of devotion charged upon their ignorance.

190. [ ] It is therefore absolutely certain that no Christian is to enter any farther into business, nor for any other ends, than such as he can in singleness of heart offer unto God, as a reasonable service.

191. [ ] Another is deaf to all the motives of piety, by indulging an idle, slothful temper.

192. [ ] Fulvius has had a learned education, and taken his degrees in the university; he came from thence, that he might be free from any rules of life.

193. [ ] He will undertake no business that may hurry his spirits, or break in upon his hours of eating and rest.

194. [ ] All people that have ever made any reflections upon what passes in their own hearts, must know that they are mighty changeable in regard to devotion.

195. [ ] An education which is not wholly intent upon this, is as much beside the point, as an art of physic that had little or no regard to the restoration of health.

196. [ ] These reasons never vary or change, they always continue in the full force; and therefore equally oblige at all times, and in regard to all persons.

## Jonathan Edwards — Religious Affections (1746)  (17)

197. [ ] The way to learn the true nature of anything, is to go where that thing is to be found in its purity and perfection.

198. [ ] As was observed before, such is our nature, and such are the laws of union of soul and body, that the mind can have no lively or vigorous exercise, without some effect upon the body.

199. [ ] Things that are very small, we cannot clearly discern their form, or distinguish them one from another; though, as they are in themselves, their form may be very different.

200. [ ] Though such things may attend what is spiritual, and be mixed with it, yet in themselves they have nothing that is spiritual, nor are they any part of gracious experience.

201. [ ] The former rejoices in himself; self is the first foundation of his joy: the latter rejoices in God.

202. [ ] In this latter respect it is that the true saints have their hearts affected, and love captivated by the free grace of God in the first place.

203. [ ] Innumerable are the ways by which the mind might be led on to all kind of evil thoughts, by exciting external ideas in the imagination.

204. [ ] The deceitfulness of the heart of man appears in no one thing so much as this of spiritual pride and self-righteousness.

205. [ ] The very reason why such outward acts, and such inward exercises, look like great abasement in such a one, is because he has a high conceit of himself.

206. [ ] The tenderness of the heart of a true Christian, is elegantly signified by our Savior, in his comparing such a one to a little child.

207. [ ] Jacob was retired for secret prayer, when Christ came to him, and he wrestled with him, and obtained the blessing.

208. [ ] What makes men partial in religion is, that they seek themselves, and not God, in their religion; and close with religion, not for its own excellent nature, but only to serve a turn.

209. [ ] A true and saving coming to Christ, is (as Christ often teaches) a coming so as to forsake all for him.

210. [ ] Love is an affection, but will any Christian say, men ought not to love God and Jesus Christ in a high degree?

211. [ ] There is something that is seen, that is wonderful; and that sight brings with it a strong conviction of something vastly beyond, that is not immediately seen.

212. [ ] As Moses' face not only shone while he was in the mount, extraordinarily conversing with God, but it continued to shine after he came down from the mount.

213. [ ] It is no barren thing; there is nothing in the universe that in its nature has a greater tendency to fruit.
